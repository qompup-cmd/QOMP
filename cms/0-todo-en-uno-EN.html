Research lines

Theoretical and computational research on light–matter interaction in gas and solid phases, driven by lasers in the terahertz and mid-infrared range.

Attosecond physics and strong fields

Ultrafast electron dynamics in atoms, molecules and solids: ionization, attosecond time delays and the strong-field approximation (1 as = 10⁻¹⁸ s).

High-order harmonic generation in solids

Harmonic emission as an all-optical spectroscopy of electronic structure: semiconductor Bloch equations and the Wannier–Bloch approach.

Quantum and topological materials

2D and 3D topological insulators (Bi₂Se₃), Chern insulators, Dirac, Weyl and multi-Weyl semimetals, Kagome lattices and quantum Hall phases; optical detection of topological phase transitions.

Two-dimensional materials

Graphene, hexagonal boron nitride and transition-metal dichalcogenides (MoS₂, WS₂, WSe₂): layer, symmetry and valley effects in the nonlinear optical response.

Spin and optical phase control

Materials with strong spin–orbit coupling: manipulation of spin currents and optical control of conductor–insulator transitions.

High-performance computing

Numerical solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation in 1D–3D, C/C++ with MPI and OpenMP, Matlab, Mathematica and Python.

Ongoing research projects of the group and the leader's past participation in international projects.

Ongoing

Helicity control of high-order harmonics

Delayed orthogonal pulses with sub-femtosecond resolution to control the polarization of harmonic emission in solids. Results submitted to Physical Review Letters (2026).

High-harmonic spectroscopy of higher-order topological insulators

Optical classification of lower- and higher-order topological phases via harmonic generation. Results submitted to Physical Review B (2025), with group students as first authors.

High-order harmonics in Weyl semimetals and Kagome lattices

Ongoing B.Sc. theses on TaAs, multi-Weyl semimetals and topological Kagome materials, in international collaboration.

Facilities

We are a theoretical-computational group: our laboratory is high-performance computing.

💻 In-house codes

Numerical solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation in 1D, 2D and 3D (Crank–Nicolson, spectral split-operator, Runge–Kutta), and semiconductor Bloch equations for the optical response of solids.

⚙️ Parallel computing

Object-oriented C/C++ with MPI and OpenMP; analysis and prototyping in Matlab, Mathematica and Python; scientific visualization with VisIt and the Silo library.

🌎 International collaborations

Joint work with the Max Planck Institute for Quantum Optics, SLAC/Stanford, Los Alamos National Laboratory, ICFO (Barcelona), the University of Central Florida and Ohio State University, among others.

Quantum Optics and Condensed Matter Physics (QOMP)

Welcome to the QOMP group, led by Prof. Dr. Alexis Chacón at the University of Panama. We explore microscopic physics from gas- to solid-state phases: attosecond science applied to condensed matter physics.

We are a small group, yet highly motivated to tackle ambitious projects in the physics of the microscopic world: quantum physics applied to optical phenomena and to condensed matter, from solids to semiconductors.

Mission

To study, control and characterize quantum materials with the tools of attosecond physics, and to propose new materials of this kind: those exhibiting the quantum Hall, quantum spin Hall or quantum anomalous Hall effects, among others.

Vision

To promote attosecond physics as a tool for the study of quantum materials and condensed matter at large: predicting new materials, optically controlling conductor–insulator phase transitions and manipulating spin currents, among other goals.
